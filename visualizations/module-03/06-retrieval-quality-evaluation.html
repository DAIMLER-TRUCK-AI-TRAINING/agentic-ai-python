Topic 6

Evals are tests for the retriever, too

You cannot tune what you do not measure. Six labelled test queries, each with the chunks a reviewer marked relevant, turn “retrieval feels fine” into numbers. Slide k (the number of chunks sent to the model) from 1 to 10 and watch precision, recall, MRR, answer relevance and the context bill pull against each other. Then switch re-ranking on and find the k you would ship.

precision@krecall@k MRR@kanswer relevance (1–5, right axis)

The retrieval eval rubric

MetricQuestion it answersHow to computeExample bar
Precision@kHow much of what I send is useful?relevant in top k ÷ k≥ 0.5 at the k you ship
Recall@kDid I send everything needed?relevant in top k ÷ all relevant for the query≥ 0.9; the most important retrieval metric for RAG
Hit rate@kDid at least one useful chunk arrive?share of queries with ≥ 1 relevant in top k1.0 on the critical queries
MRR@kHow high is the first useful chunk?mean of 1 ÷ rank of the first relevant chunk≥ 0.8 when you send few chunks
Answer relevanceDoes the generated answer address the question?a judge model scores each answer 1–5 against a rubric; spot-check by people≥ 4.0 mean
Context tokensWhat does each answer cost and how long does it take?k × average chunk tokenswithin the latency and cost budget

Diagnosing a low score

SymptomLikely causeTry
Low recall@10, even with a large kThe first stage never finds the chunkChunking (Topic 4), hybrid search for codes and IDs (Topic 5), query rewriting, a better embedding model
Good recall@10, low recall@3Relevant chunks are found but ranked lowA re-ranker over the top 20–50, metadata boosts
Low precision at the k you shipk is larger than the answers needA smaller k after re-ranking; a relevance threshold
Good retrieval, low answer relevanceGeneration: the prompt, the model, or noise in the contextPrompt and citation rules; fewer, better chunks; check groundedness (Module 5)
Scores differ by query typeOne method suits one kind of questionSlice the eval set by type (IDs, how-to, policy) and report each slice
Engineering takeaway: build the labelled set before you tune anything: 30–100 real questions from tickets, each with the chunk IDs a subject-matter expert marks relevant. Re-run it on every change to chunking, embeddings, k, filters or re-ranking, exactly like a regression suite. Groundedness and faithfulness evaluation of the answers comes in Module 5.
Common trap: raising k until recall looks good. Recall climbs, but precision falls, every answer pays for more tokens, and answer relevance drops once irrelevant chunks crowd the context. And no k recovers a chunk the first stage never retrieved: TQ-06’s escalation section is missing at every k, which is a chunking or query problem, not a k problem.
